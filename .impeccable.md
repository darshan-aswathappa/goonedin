## Design Context

### Users
Power users — a mix of data-savvy job seekers and recruiters who want deep job market intelligence. They're comfortable with dense, technical interfaces and come here to find signals others don't have. They use this in a focused, purposeful mode: scanning for actionable insights, not casual browsing.

### Brand Personality
**Authoritative. Precise. Relentless.**

The interface should feel like a Bloomberg terminal operated by someone who knows exactly what they're looking for. Information density is a feature, not a problem. Every pixel should earn its place.

Emotional goal: *Command and authority* — "I have information others don't, and I'm in control."

### Aesthetic Direction
Bloomberg Terminal meets modern data journalism. Stark black backgrounds, amber/orange as the primary signal color (already established via `--teal: #ff8c00`), monospace JetBrains Mono typography, sharp 2px corners, no gradients, no soft shadows.

**Anti-references**: Avoid anything that looks like a SaaS dashboard, a consumer analytics tool, or a startup product. No cards with colored icons, no gradients, no rounded corners beyond 2px.

**Theme**: Dark mode only. The existing color system is correct — do not introduce new colors.

### Design Principles
1. **Rhythm over uniformity** — Varied spacing communicates hierarchy. Equal padding everywhere reads as no hierarchy.
2. **Density is a virtue** — This audience wants more information, not less. Breathe between sections, not within them.
3. **The hero chart leads** — Every tab has one primary visualization. Give it the most vertical space and let secondary charts recede.
4. **Terminal authenticity** — Empty states, loading states, and transitions should feel like terminal output, not UI feedback.
5. **One brand color, used with discipline** — Amber/orange (#ff8c00) signals what matters. Never dilute it by using it on everything.
